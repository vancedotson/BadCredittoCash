# Brand Voice & Writing Style Guide — Vance Dotson / Bad Credit to Cash

**Prepared for:** Sales page + webinar funnel + all campaign copy
**Companions:** `target-audience-avatar.md`, `offer-detailed.md`
**Purpose:** So every word sounds like Vance and lands with a skeptical, harassed, working-class audience — not like generic funnel hype.

> **Compliance guardrails carried into the voice (safe mode):** plain-spoken and confident, never guarantee-y. We anchor to *inaccurate/incomplete information* and *holding them accountable under federal law*. Lines that depend on Vance's attorney status are marked ⚠️. When in doubt, the voice picks the truthful, provable version — because for this audience, *credible* beats *impressive* every time.

---

## 1. The voice in one line

> **A straight-talking veteran fighter who's on your side — calm, plain-spoken, and done watching good people get bullied.**

Not a salesman. Not a guru. Not a soft, therapeutic helper. Vance is the person who's seen this for 20 years, isn't impressed by the bureaus or the collectors, and will tell you the truth plainly — then go to work.

---

## 2. Who's speaking (the persona)

**Vance is the advocate in your corner.** The nearest archetypes: the tough-but-fair coach, the union rep who knows the rulebook cold, the neighbor who happens to be an expert and won't let you get ripped off. He's:

- **Been there since 2004** — nothing shocks him, and that's reassuring
- **On offense, not defense** — he doesn't ask nicely, he holds people accountable
- **Plain-spoken** — explains the law like he's talking to you at a kitchen table, not a courtroom
- **Righteous, not preachy** — his anger is *on your behalf*, aimed at the bureaus and collectors, never at you
- **Unshakably calm** — the calm of someone who already knows how this ends

**He is NOT:** hyper, hype-y, salesy, jargon-y, condescending, or falsely cheerful.

---

## 3. The audience's emotional state (and the tone that meets it)

You are almost never writing to a neutral reader. You're writing to someone in one of these states — meet them where they are:

| Their emotional state | What they're feeling | The tone that meets it |
|---|---|---|
| **Harassed** | Anxious, jumpy, phone-dread | Steady and protective: "That stops now." |
| **Ashamed** | "I'm responsible — how did I end up here?" | Zero judgment; reframe: *you're not the problem* |
| **Bullied / powerless** | Small, steamrolled, no way to fight back | Righteous strength: *someone's finally on your side* |
| **Burned / distrustful** | "I paid for help before and got nothing" | Proof-first, plain, no hype; earn it |
| **Skeptical** | "Is this a scam? Is this even legal?" | Calm, factual, receipts on the table |
| **Quietly hopeful** | *Wants* to believe it's possible | Confident but honest — never over-promise |

**The golden rule:** validate the *feeling*, never amplify the *despair*. We name the anger and the exhaustion, then immediately pivot to agency and action. We do not wallow.

---

## 4. Voice principles (the five pillars)

**1. Plain over polished.**
Kitchen-table English. If a word wouldn't come up in a normal conversation, cut it.
- ✅ "The stuff on your report that isn't even right."
- ❌ "Inaccurate adverse tradelines furnished by data furnishers."

**2. Their side, always.**
Every line makes clear who the enemy is — the bureaus and the collectors — and that Vance is *with* the reader against them.
- ✅ "They broke the rules. Let's make them answer for it."

**3. Confident, never boastful; honest, never hype.**
Strength comes from *proof and plainness*, not exclamation points and superlatives. This audience is allergic to hype because hype is what scammed them.
- ✅ "I've been doing this since 2004. Here's a call where a collector got caught."
- ❌ "The #1 CREDIT SECRET banks DON'T want you to know!!!"

**4. Dignity first.**
Never make the reader feel stupid, judged, or lectured. They already feel small — the copy makes them feel *backed up*.
- ✅ "You did nothing wrong. The system did."

**5. Show, don't claim.**
For a burned audience, *proof out-converts adjectives*. Lead with the recorded call, the real result, the real person — let those carry the weight.

---

## 5. Tone calibration across the funnel

The voice is one person, but the dial moves by stage:

- **Cold ad (meet the anger):** short, punchy, emotionally direct. Name the pain in their own words. Job = the click, nothing more.
  *"They're calling your job now? That's illegal. And it can stop."*
- **Webinar (authority + proof):** warmer, teacherly, generous. Vance explains the mechanism, shows the receipts, dissolves the "scam/legal/already-tried" objections. Job = trust + book the call.
- **Call CTA (safety + warmth):** low-pressure, reassuring, removes risk. Job = make saying "yes" feel safe.
  *"The call's free. Worst case, you find out where you stand. Best case, we go to work."*
- **Nurture emails:** conversational, one idea each, like a text from someone who's got your back.

## 6. What BOOSTS CONFIDENCE (use liberally)

These are the levers that make a skeptical reader believe:

- **A real, named human** — "Vance," first person, "I," a face, an office, a phone number
- **Specificity** — "since 2004," "a bankruptcy and two medical collections deleted," "a call where the collector admits it" — specifics feel true; round claims feel fake
- **Receipts** — recorded collector calls, video testimonials, documented results
- **Plain explanation of the mechanism** — when they understand *how* it works, it stops feeling like magic (i.e., a scam)
- **Naming the enemy precisely** — "TransUnion, Equifax, Experian" and "third-party collectors" — precision signals expertise
- **"You could do this yourself, but…"** — honesty about DIY paradoxically builds trust (and it's CROA-favored)
- **Calm certainty** — the tone of someone who's seen this a thousand times

---

## 7. What LOWERS THE ACTION THRESHOLD (use at CTAs)

What makes them actually book:

- **"Free."** The call/webinar costs nothing — say it plainly, more than once
- **"No judgment."** Explicitly remove the shame barrier — "I've seen it all, none of it surprises me"
- **A safe, small first step** — "just a conversation," "just find out if you have a case," "no obligation"
- **The reframe: *they may owe YOU.*** This flips the whole emotional weight from debtor-shame to righteous-hope — the single most action-triggering idea in the arsenal ⚠️ (keep tied to "where the law allows")
- **"It can stop right away."** The speed of harassment relief is a powerful now-lever
- **Removing the "is it legal" fear** — "this is just enforcing laws that already exist to protect you"
- **Genuine, honest urgency** — the calls continue every day you wait; some claims are time-sensitive ⚠️

## 8. Sound bites & swipe lines

Ready-to-adapt lines that carry the voice. **Compliant as written unless marked ⚠️** (⚠️ = confirm mechanism/attorney status first).

**Pain / hook:**
- "That feeling when your phone buzzes and your stomach drops? That ends."
- "They're calling your job. Your family. At dinner. That's not just rude — a lot of it is illegal."
- "You already tried disputing. That's exactly why it didn't work."
- "Half the stuff on your report isn't even right — and you know it."

**Differentiation:**
- ⚠️ "Most 'credit repair' sends weak letters and hopes. I hold them accountable."
- ⚠️ "I don't just dispute. I go after them."
- "Anybody can mail a letter. Knowing the law is the difference."

**The reframe:**
- ⚠️ "You've been thinking about what you owe them. Let's talk about what they might owe *you*."
- "For once, you're not the one on defense."

**Trust / authority:**
- "I've been doing this since 2004. Let me show you a recorded call."
- "You don't have to trust me. Trust the receipts."
- "Real person. Real office. Real cases. Not a call center."

**Reassurance / CTA:**
- "The call's free and there's no judgment. I've seen it all."
- "Worst case, you find out exactly where you stand. That alone is worth an hour."
- "You're not the problem. The system broke its own rules. Let's use them."

**Empowerment close:**
- "You've been carrying this alone. You don't have to anymore."
- "Stop absorbing it. Start answering back."

## 10. What they DON'T want to hear (avoid)

**Tone landmines (make them feel worse):**
- **Anything that judges or lectures.** No "you should have," no "if you'd just budgeted," no finger-wagging about financial responsibility. They already feel this. It kills trust instantly.
- **Toxic positivity / cheerleading.** "Just stay positive!" reads as clueless to someone being harassed.
- **Corporate/robotic distance.** "We value your inquiry." No. This is a person talking to a person.
- **Hype and hard-sell.** Caps-lock urgency, fake countdowns, "SECRET the banks HATE" — this audience was *scammed by that voice*. It's a trust-killer here specifically.
- **Pity.** They want an ally, not sympathy. "You poor thing" is the wrong register; "That's not right, and we can do something about it" is the right one.
- **Jargon.** Metro 2, furnishers, tradelines, adverse items — not in customer-facing copy.

**Compliance landmines (make them illegal — do not use):**
- ❌ Guaranteed **"deletions"** of negative items generally, or promising to remove **accurate** information
- ❌ Guaranteed **score increases** ("boost your score 100 points," "get to 750")
- ❌ "Erase bad credit," "wipe your report clean," "delete anything"
- ❌ Guaranteed **outcomes** of any kind ("we'll get it removed," "guaranteed results")
- ❌ Anything implying **government affiliation/approval** or affiliation with the bureaus
- ❌ ⚠️ Language implying Vance **personally represents you as your lawyer** unless attorney status is confirmed
- ❌ "New credit identity," CPN, or anything advising misrepresentation

**Safe substitutes:**
| Don't say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "We'll delete your bad credit" | "We challenge the information that's inaccurate" |
| "Guaranteed to raise your score" | "We hold them accountable under the law" ⚠️ |
| "Erase your debt" | "Find out if it's even valid — and if they broke the rules" |
| "We're your lawyers" ⚠️ | "We're your advocate in this fight" |
| "100% removal guaranteed" | "The strategy call is free — you'll leave knowing where you stand" |

## 11. Mechanics & style rules

- **Person:** First person for Vance ("I," "me"), second person for the reader ("you," "your"). Warm, direct, one-to-one. Minimize "we/our" corporate distance except where it signals a team behind him.
- **Reading level:** ~6th–8th grade. Short sentences. Short paragraphs (1–3 lines). Lots of white space.
- **Rhythm:** Vary length. Land hard points as **short standalone lines.** Like this.
- **Punctuation:** Periods do the work. Use exclamation points almost never (they read as hype). Em-dashes and short fragments for a spoken, human rhythm.
- **Questions:** Use rhetorical questions that mirror their inner monologue ("Sound familiar?").
- **Specifics over adjectives:** "since 2004" beats "very experienced"; a named result beats "amazing results."
- **Every section earns the next:** hook → agitate (briefly, without wallowing) → reframe → proof → safe CTA.
- **CTA style:** low-pressure, benefit-first, free-forward. "Book your free call" > "Submit."

## 12. Before → after rewrites (make it concrete)

**Ex 1 — hero line**
- ❌ *"Our proven credit repair system will boost your score fast and delete negative items — guaranteed!"* (hype + illegal claims)
- ✅ *"They keep calling. Half your report isn't even right. Let's do something about both — and find out if they owe you."* ⚠️

**Ex 2 — trust builder**
- ❌ *"We are a leading provider of credit restoration solutions."* (corporate, vague)
- ✅ *"I'm Vance. I've been fighting for people like you since 2004. Here's a recorded call where a collector got caught breaking the law."*

**Ex 3 — CTA**
- ❌ *"Sign up now — limited time offer, act fast!"* (fake urgency, scam-adjacent)
- ✅ *"Book a free call. No cost, no judgment. Worst case, you find out exactly where you stand."*

**Ex 4 — objection handling**
- ❌ *"Don't worry, it's totally legal, trust us!"* (protests too much)
- ✅ *"Is this legal? It's the opposite of a trick — it's enforcing the federal laws that already exist to protect you. Most people just never knew they could."*

---

## 13. The voice test (before anything ships)

Read the copy and ask:
1. Does it sound like **one plain-spoken person** talking to another — or like a brand?
2. Would a **harassed, skeptical** reader feel *backed up* — or *sold to*?
3. Is every strong claim **backed by proof** or truthfully hedged — or is it hype?
4. Did we **name the enemy** and put Vance on the reader's side?
5. Does it **validate the feeling without wallowing** in it?
6. Does it clear **every compliance landmine** in §10?

If any answer is no, it's not on-voice yet.
