# The webinar script — Vance Dotson (belief ladder)

The webinar is a **decision event, not a training** (ebook §3). It doesn't sell
because it teaches; it sells because it shifts belief. This is the belief ladder in
the ebook's twelve blocks (§3.4), written for Vance's free-call offer.

Timings assume a ~35 to 45 minute evergreen cut (tighter than the 90-minute live
template). Vance records this; the room player is a placeholder until then.

**Voice:** plain, warm, no jargon, no hype. **Compliance:** no guaranteed results, no
money-back %, no fake urgency. `⚠️` marks where real proof must be dropped in.

---

## Block 1 — Hook (0:00–2:00)

Big result + the tradeoff removed + the unique mechanism + who it's for. Lock
attention in the first two minutes or the rest is wasted.

> If collectors are calling and there's stuff on your credit report that shouldn't
> be there, the next 35 minutes are for you. I'm going to show you how the calls
> actually stop, and how to find out if the people who broke the rules owe *you*. Not
> weak dispute letters. The two federal laws that were already on your side the whole
> time. You don't need to be an expert. You just need to see what I'm about to show
> you.

## Block 2 — Frame control (2:00–4:00)

Set the rules, build anticipation, pre-frame the offer so there's no resistance later.

> Watch to the end. The part most people need, what to actually do about your own
> report, comes near the end. Stay with me. And at the end, if it makes sense, I'll
> show you the shortcut: a free call where I look at your specific situation. No
> pressure. First, let me show you why this even works.

## Block 3 — Problem + pain (4:00–8:00)

Make the problem real, inevitable, expensive. Don't teach yet.

> Your phone buzzes and your stomach drops before you even look. They've called your
> job. Your family. At dinner. There's stuff on your report that isn't even yours and
> it won't come off. You disputed it and it came back "verified". Maybe you paid a
> credit-repair company and got nothing. Here's the part that matters: none of that
> means you're stuck. It means the rules got broken.

## Block 4 — Destroy the old solutions (8:00–12:00)

Break what they've tried. "It's not your fault."

> DIY disputes and cheap credit-repair mills all do the same thing: they mail letters
> and hope. So the bureau stamps it "verified" and everyone gives up. It's not your
> fault. The system you were told to use is built to wear you down. Sending a letter
> and hoping was never going to move people who only respond to one thing: the law.

## Block 5 — New mechanism (12:00–18:00)

The big idea. Name it, define it, prove it's different. "Okay, this makes sense."

> Here's the difference. I don't send a letter and hope. I build what I call **the
> Violation Ledger**. Every harassing call, every inaccurate line on your report,
> logged as what it legally is: a violation of the FCRA or the FDCPA. Those are two
> federal laws. The FCRA says the bureaus have to report the truth. The FDCPA says
> collectors can't harass you. When they break those rules, that ledger turns your
> complaint into leverage.

## Block 6 — Teach the framework (18:00–26:00)

Enough value to build belief, not so much they don't need you. Give the map, not
every footstep.

> Three moves. **One, find the violations.** I go through your reports and the
> collector activity for what's inaccurate, incomplete, or against the law. **Two,
> hold them accountable under the law.** Not a letter, a demand grounded in the
> statute. **Three, push for results and, where the law allows, the compensation you
> may be owed.** That's the whole method. You could learn to do this yourself. Most
> people would rather have someone who's done it since 2004.

## Block 7 — Proof + case studies (26:00–31:00)

Two to four stories. Validate the outcome, the mechanism, the speed, the "someone
like me". `⚠️ Use only real, documented, permissioned examples.`

> ⚠️ [Recorded call: a collector crossing the FDCPA line, on tape.]
> ⚠️ [A report where inaccurate items were challenged and removed.]
> ⚠️ [A client, in their words, on the calls stopping.]
> Don't take my word for it. Listen to them get caught.

## Block 8 — Transition (31:00–33:00)

The clean move. Never "now I'll pitch". This is where most people crash.

> Now you know the system. If you want to implement it faster and skip the mistakes,
> here's the shortcut. I'll take a look at your specific situation myself, and tell
> you straight whether you have a case.

*(This is where the room reveals the "Book my free strategy call" CTA.)*

## Block 9 — Offer stack (33:00–38:00)

Make it impossible to say "I don't know what I'm getting". Here the "offer" is the
free call, so stack the *value of the call*, not a price.

> On the call: we review the calls you're getting and the items on your report. We
> find out whether there's a violation to hold them to. And you leave knowing exactly
> where you stand, either way. It's free. There's no obligation. Its only job is to
> tell you whether you even have a case before you decide anything.

## Block 10 — Objections (38:00–41:00)

Handle before the close. Time, money, "is this a scam", "will it work for me".

> "Is this a scam?" I'm a real, named advocate, out of a real office in Oklahoma City,
> since 2004. Trust the recorded calls, not me. "Is it even legal?" It's the opposite
> of a trick, it's enforcing laws that already exist to protect you. "What will it
> cost?" The call is free. "Will I be judged?" Never. You're not the problem here.

## Block 11 — Close (41:00–44:00)

Honest urgency, repetition. No fake timers. Repeat the CTA.

> Every day you wait, the calls keep coming and the wrong information keeps costing
> you. Some legal claims are also time-sensitive. It's just me, so I only open a
> limited number of calls a week. If the calls are yours and the errors are yours,
> book the free call and let's look. Worst case, you find out exactly where you stand.

## Block 12 — Q&A (optional)

Not "help". More objection-handling and reassurance, in disguise. Keep steering back
to: book the call, it's free, you'll know where you stand.

---

## The five beliefs this ladder installs (ebook §3.1)

1. They believe **you** (real advocate, recorded proof).
2. They believe the **outcome is possible** (the results, the mechanism).
3. They believe **your method is the right path** (the Violation Ledger, not letters).
4. They believe **they can do it** (with you, it's simple: book a call).
5. They believe **now is the time** (calls keep coming, some claims are timed).

> "It's not your fault" does more work than any slide. Relief opens people up.
